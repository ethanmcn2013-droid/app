FINDING id=ended-screen-says-the-day-was
Seat: Brand and copy
Element: states.ended — .b-endedTitle, .b-endedBody, .b-note (render-b.js:1486-1491)
Problem: The screen an audience reaches when the link is turned off is the only screen in the product that is factually wrong, and the only one that speaks in a fault register. "The day was Saturday 3 October 2026." is hardcoded past tense with no reference to the clock, so a link switched off in July tells the couple's family that the most important day of their life is over. The headline "This link has stopped working." names a malfunction, and it contradicts the promise the owner was given one screen earlier on publish — "Anyone holding it then sees that it has ended" — so one state carries two names and the product ships the harsher one. It then spends its third sentence walking the alarm back, repeating "stopped working" a second time in four lines: "Nothing has been deleted. Only the link stopped working." Three sentences, and two of them exist to undo the damage the first one did.
Proposed fix: Headline becomes "This link has ended." — the word publish already uses for this state. Body branches on the clock through the accessor already in the file: where daysFrom(F.today, F.project.primaryDate.date) > 0 render "The day is " + F.fmt.longYear(...) + ". Ask " + F.workspace.owner + " for a new link."; at 0 or below keep "The day was ". Third line becomes "Nothing has been deleted." — delete the clause "Only the link stopped working."
