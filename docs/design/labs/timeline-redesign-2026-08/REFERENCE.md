## What the before actually does

Read without loyalty, because nothing here binds.

- **The owner surface is a dashboard wearing a magazine's clothes.** A 96px
  name, a one-line subtitle, then a horizontal time strip with labels floating
  above and below the line. The strip is the only real idea on the page and it
  is asked to carry nine milestones in a space that fits four, so four of them
  collapse into an unlabelled "4 milestones" bracket.
- **Two clocks disagree in tone.** "79 days" sits top-right as a telemetry
  readout; "79 days to go" repeats in the breadcrumb; "Wedding day · 3 Oct"
  appears three times on one screen in three grammars.
- **The count is arguable.** "2 of 9 complete" over a fixture of ten
  milestones — the cancelled one is silently dropped rather than stated. A
  guest cannot reconstruct the number from what is on screen.
- **The received artifact is a rail with a list beside it.** Proportional
  spacing is the right instinct and it produces four hundred pixels of empty
  rail between January and April on a phone. Nothing about it says *wedding*;
  swap the strings and it is a product roadmap.
- **There is no ceremony.** Publishing is a "Share" button and a page headed
  "See the story they will see." The moment a private plan becomes someone
  else's copy is a form.
- **Chrome leaks.** The owner's initials render as "DO" (Demo Operator) on the
  publication page — the fixture-name leak the method's lesson library names by
  class. The in-development pill sits over the content in every frame.
- **The day itself does not exist.** Nothing in the product changes on
  3 October. The print route exists but is not composed as a keepsake.

That is the honest start: a competent, generic project surface serving the most
emotionally loaded document most of its users will ever send.
