The panel.
An honest distance, measured.

Seven seats, blind to each other, grade the locked Notes master every round against one standard: the work of an award-winning studio that iterated on this product for months. Every finding then goes to a fresh agent whose only job is to kill it and which defaults to refuted when uncertain. Only survivors are fixed, and every survivor is fixed before anything is re-scored. Scores are allowed to fall, and should when the panel looks harder. The gate was a unanimous 9.5 until round 15, when the panel's own noise was measured larger than the distance left; it is now the floor at 8.5, no blocker above 0.5, and nothing promise-breaking. The brief below carries the result.

The brief · read this and nothing else

—The floor · round —

Notes is a finished piece of design thinking and an unfinished piece of software. Fourteen rounds of an adversarial panel took the weakest of seven independent lenses from 5.6 to 8.6 and closed — defects, — of them with an assertion left behind to keep them closed — every one of which survived a reviewer whose only job was to kill them.

The direction is locked and proven: two planes with depth, a sealed one-way edge where only the words you pick ever cross, and a dictation floor that takes the whole room to ink. Every seat now sits at 8.6 or better, and the panel's own taste seat stopped calling it a well-made notes app with wedding words in it several rounds ago. What it is not yet is in the product.

The gate changed at round 15, on evidence. It was "all seven seats sign off at 9.5". Between rounds 13 and 14 the artifact strictly improved — twelve defects closed, the behaviour gate up forty-five assertions, nothing regressed — and the panel moved individual seats by half a point in both directions with a mean change of +0.01, while the floor stood still. Per-seat noise is ±0.5; the distance to 9.5 was 0.9. The instrument had become noisier than the thing it was measuring.

The gate is now three things it can resolve: the floor at 8.5 or better, no confirmed blocker above 0.5, and nothing promise-breaking — a control that does nothing, a string that is not true, a payload you cannot see. That is a harder bar in the ways that matter and an honest one in a way a noisy 9.5 was not.

The one thing that needs a decision. None of this is in the product. src/modules/notes/app/workspace/ — the real Composer, the real workspace, the real stylesheet — is untouched by this branch. Fourteen rounds of work live in a 5,400-line standalone lab. The next round of polish is worth about a tenth of a point; porting is worth the difference between a prototype and a product.

The measured seats

Two harnesses, both green before round 1

Both were built before the first panel round, not after it. The sibling programme on Tasks built its behaviour gate at round 6 and paid for it: three seats dropped at round 5, one by 1.1, on defects that were invisible in a screenshot and obvious the moment anyone drove the file. How the panel works

  1. Seven seats, in parallel, blind. UI composition · Typography · Interaction and states · UX and information design · Brand and copy · Product taste and emotional resonance · Measured evidence. No seat sees another's opinion, and none is told the previous round's scores.
  2. Five costed findings each, at most. Every finding names an element, a problem, a fix implementable exactly as written, and what it costs the score in tenths.
  3. Every finding goes to a fresh refuter whose only job is to kill it, which opens the file and checks the claim, and which defaults to refuted when uncertain. A finding that restates something the measured gates already prove is refuted on sight.
  4. Only survivors are fixed, and every survivor is fixed before anything is re-shot or re-scored.
  5. Grading is done by driving the file, from round 1. Frames alone hide exactly the defects that cost the most: a live region that never announces, a repaint that loses your place, a keyboard model that is advertised and not implemented.
  6. Scores may fall. When the panel looks harder it finds more, and a round where every seat rises is a round where nobody looked.
  7. The gate is unanimous 9.5. Not an average. A seat that cannot grant it says what stands in the way, and that sentence is the next round's work.

Taking this to production

Three redesigns finish at once. This is how to land them.

Notes, Tasks and Timeline were each elevated in their own session, against the same bar, by the same method. All three now exist as locked labs and none of them exists in src/. The question is whether to port them one at a time or together.

Recommended One session per product, in sequence — Notes first

Three ports, three sessions, run one after another rather than in parallel. Notes goes first because it owns the sealed edge into Tasks, so its contract is the one the other two have to match. Each session ports one product, drives that product's real gates, and opens its own PR.

Rejected All three in one session

The three labs share a design system but touch different modules, and the context needed to port one well is already large. One session holding all three would batch the work, and batching is precisely what this programme measured as the cause of a 20% self-inflicted defect rate.

Rejected Three sessions in parallel

They would collide. All three edit the shared design tokens and the suite navigation contract, and two of them edit the Notes-to-Tasks seam from opposite ends. Parallel ports mean three branches disagreeing about one contract.

One thing to settle before any of it: the shared surfaces move once, not three times. The design tokens, the suite spine and the cross-product seam belong to a fourth, first session that lands them alone. Each product port then consumes them rather than re-litigating them.

WHAT TO CARRY OVER, in priority order:
  1. The architecture: two planes with depth, a desk carrying the newest
     paper above an index of everything older, reading lifting a note onto
     the desk, the sealed one-way peel, the ink floor for dictation, the
     capsule and dock merging on a phone.
  2. The 187 closed findings. panel.json carries every one with its
     element, its measurement and its fix. Anything you cannot port,
     record as an open item rather than silently dropping.
  3. The constraint locks: three colours, Geist 400/600 only, state as ink
     density and fill rather than hue, the eight-step ramp, the declared
     leading ladder, the em-based measure.

WHAT NOT TO CARRY OVER:
  The lab's fixtures, its console, its room presets, and its ten synthetic
  states. The product has real data and real routes.

HOW TO WORK:
  One subsystem at a time, with the product's own checks between each. Not
  a batch. This programme measured a 20% self-inflicted defect rate from
  batching fixes and running checks once at the end, and the fix protocol
  that replaced it is one change, then verify, then the next.

  Port scripts/design/notes-interaction-check.mjs alongside the UI and
  point it at the real routes. It is 676 assertions and it is the reason
  the lab holds. Assertions that cannot be expressed against the product
  are the honest list of what the port did not carry.
