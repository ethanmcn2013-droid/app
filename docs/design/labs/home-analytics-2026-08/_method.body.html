Three rounds

A retrospective on the method, not the product. Written by the session that ran it — which is a conflict, and is named here rather than left for the reader to notice.

The conflict, stated before the findings

A session scoring a loop it also modified is an interested witness.

This session ran the panel, applied every fix, wrote most of the new gate assertions, and is now grading the instrument it spent three rounds sharpening. A report that only praises the method would be worthless, so the sharpest material here is the method’s failures — including four this session caused and one it shipped into a shared skill.

What held

  • The refuter is the load-bearing part. 22%, 26%, 25% — a quarter of everything seven expert seats filed did not survive one fresh reader instructed to default to refuted, and the rate was stable across three rounds and two panel sizes. Without it, one finding in four would have become a change to a surface that did not need one.
  • Refuting the fix while affirming the problem. Ten of round three’s twenty-one confirmed findings arrived this way: the defect real, the proposed repair wrong. The best example would have turned a green gate red in the closing round — a corrected regex that matches “4 May” in the chart’s own spoken line. Without this category those ten either ship a bad fix or die on a technicality.
  • Clustering before refutation. 131 findings as filed, 84 distinct claims. Convergence is recorded as evidence rather than paid for twice — three seats independently found the same dead regex, and that agreement is in the record instead of being three refutations of one thing.
  • Severity as the ending, not a score. Seven blind seats disagree with a standard deviation of 0.335, so a unanimous 9.5 would need true quality of 9.94 to pass half the time. The scores here moved 7.0→8.6 at the floor and gated nothing, correctly. The severity ledger resolved cleanly every round.
  • Assertion first, watched failing. Seven surface findings in round three were driven against the unfixed master and all seven went red before anything was repaired. Two of the seven probes were wrong when first written and were caught by that same run.

What broke

  • The gate certified eleven things it could not see. Four found in rounds 1–2, seven more in the closing round, every one of them guarding a finding already recorded closed. Four source backslashes compiling to a literal backslash. A predicate grepping document.styleSheets for selector text it never rendered. Three comparing the loading DOM to itself. A scrollBy on a container a later round stopped making a scroller. --h === 100 against a template that types --h:100. An accessible name checked for length > 12 and nothing else. An assertion that cannot fail is worse than none, because it certifies — and the only defence found was the panel itself.
  • Self-inflicted cost is the metric that decided this engagement, and it is not in the loop. Nothing in the method stops between batches to ask whether the last batch caused the next round. It is recorded at the end of a round, by hand, from a judgement about provenance — and even that was nearly guessed here. Grepping the fix scripts for each exact string, and demoting every case where a script had used a string as an anchor rather than introducing it, moved five claims from self-inflicted to not. Estimated, the number would have been wrong by a fifth.
  • The rotating three is a real gap. Round 2 sat three seats and produced the round that was not frozen and the round whose repairs caused most of round 3. Cheaper per round, more expensive across the engagement.
  • The shared report builder threw on the method’s own prescription. The round chart iterated every declared seat across every round and called .toFixed() on the result — so any engagement that follows the rotating-three rule publishes a log that dies on load, taking the ledger and every block below it with it. Fixed at source. It had almost certainly been live since round 2 and was caught only because the artifact verifier was run this time.
  • Four of five red lines in this round’s own verification were new probes measuring the wrong thing — a heading selected by tag in a state that heads with a paragraph, a selection measured with containsNode, which is blind to user-select; a concord check keyed on a row that state does not have; a click driven through a helper that scrolls its own target into view and then reads the scroll back as the defect. The discipline caught all four. It catches nothing that is not run.

What should change

  • Make self-inflicted cost a gate, not a note. It is the number that ended this engagement and it is computed last, by hand. Attribute each confirmed finding against the fix scripts as it is confirmed, and refuse to open round N+1 above the threshold.
  • Add a liveness class to the gate. Eleven vacuous assertions in one engagement is not bad luck. Every predicate built from a constructed pattern should carry a fixed probe beside it proving it still finds what it is for — the pattern used here for both repaired guards, and the only one that would have caught them without a panel.
  • Freeze earlier. The freeze arrived at round 3 by design; round 2’s restructuring is the direct cause of the pair failing. Freezing at the first clean-on-severity round would likely have closed this ledger.
  • Verify the artifacts every round, not at the close. One published document had been throwing on load and one had never carried a viewport meta — the exact defect lessons.md L-14 was written about, in the set of documents that records L-14.

Was it worth it

For the instrument, unambiguously: the behaviour gate went from 122 assertions to 506, eleven of which were certifying nothing and now cannot, and it travels with the work. For the surface, yes but with a limit that should be said rather than implied — six misleading claims and a self-contradicting quiet state are gone, and the last two rounds bought progressively less of the product and more of the loop.

The strongest argument for the method is that it reached its own stopping condition without being told to, from a metric that indicts the session running it. The strongest argument against it is the one the method already makes about itself: three engagements, forty-five rounds, 1,358 findings, 919 fixes, zero shipped pixels. This is the fourth, and it ends at a port packet rather than in the product.

Whether that is a fourth failure or the first hand-over depends entirely on what happens next, which is not this session’s to decide.
