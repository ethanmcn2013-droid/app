01

The finding that unlocks the view

“No trend is producible” conflated two different things. Separating them is what lets this view have a chart at all.

A state trend needs a snapshot

“How much was open last Tuesday” is a question about a moment that has passed. Nobody wrote the answer down at the time, and it cannot be reconstructed. It needs the snapshot writer, which exists but has never run. This plan refuses every state trend until snapshots have accrued.

An event trend needs only the event’s own timestamp

“How much did I finish each week” is a question about events that each carry their own date. tasks.completed_at is the durable completion moment (migration 0025, providers/tasks.ts:158). And the machinery to bucket it already exists and is already careful:

buildTrendPoints() declares work_completed a trend metric, buckets it at seven days when the period is 93 days or under, and skips any bucket not wholly inside the provider’s covered window rather than zero-filling it.

trend-series.ts:13–16, 55, 62 — “A partial bucket could contain records but cannot safely represent zeroes for the uncovered part of its interval.”

An 84-day period is twelve weekly buckets. That is the chart in §04, and it needs no new infrastructure, no cron, and no flag beyond the view’s own. The honesty is not something the design adds — it is already in the function.

02

The question it exists for

“Am I actually getting on top of this, or am I just busy?”

The two behind it: What has quietly slipped while I wasn’t looking? and Is this stretch better or worse than my normal?

Home already has two tenses and this is the third. That is the whole argument for where it sits and why it does not compete with what is already there:

Present Today’s Signal — what needs me right now? Three rows, ranked, at /app/home.
Today Full briefing — what crossed a line today? The read of record, at /app/home/briefing.
Past Lately — how have I been doing, and what has gone quiet? At /app/home/analytics.

Nothing in Lately is urgent. That is deliberate and it is the whole reason it can be a separate tab rather than another module on the front door: an owner opens Today’s Signal every morning under time pressure, and opens Lately on a Friday afternoon with a coffee. Two different readers of the same data, and the composition serves the second one.

The name

The route is /app/home/analytics and the internal name stays Analytics. The tab says Lately.

A venue owner does not say “analytics”, and the suite’s audience is the 80% not in tech (docs/FIRST_CONTACT_TEST.md). The naming contract governs product names, and Home is explicitly not marketed as a product (SUITE_URL_AND_NAMING_CONTRACT.md:18), so a plain-English tab label inside it breaches nothing. analytics.signalstudio.ie already redirects to a marketing page, which is further evidence the word is not free as a product noun.

This is the one decision in the plan that needs the founder rather than the evidence. If “Lately” is wrong, the composition is unaffected — only the word changes.

04

The composition, and why that order

Three movements. The eye hits a very large number first, and that is the argument.

Movement one
What you did

The hero numeral and the twelve-week chart. Answers the question the tab exists for, before the reader has scrolled.

Movement two
What’s quietly slipping

Four counts and the ages strip. The thing Today’s Signal structurally cannot show.

Movement three
What this can’t tell you

The refusals, at full section weight. Not a footnote and not an apology.

Why “what you did” comes first

Every existing surface in Signal Studio is a demand. Tasks shows what is undone. Timeline shows what is coming. Today’s Signal shows what needs you. Nothing in the product has ever told an owner they did well, and an owner who has just cleared their best four weeks since spring deserves to be told so before being handed a list of problems.

It is also the honest order. The completions count is the most trustworthy number on the page — observed, no history requirement, every one of it openable. Leading with the strongest evidence and descending to the weakest is the shape of a good report.

Why movement two earns the view’s existence

Today’s Signal ranks by what crossed a line today. Work that is quietly slipping crosses no line — it just sits. A job untouched for 41 days generates no event, has no due date passing, and triggers no rule. It is invisible to the front door by construction. That gap is the reason this tab is not a duplicate of the briefing.

How it sits beside the other two

A tab strip under the Home header, three items, aria-current="page" on the active one, real links so it works without JavaScript. Today’s Signal stays the default and the dominant module; nothing about /app/home changes except that a third tab appears. Lately gets no rail icon, no wordmark, no marketing page — it is a view, not a product.

05

The charts, and the colour rule the suite inherits

Two charts ship. Both were chosen with the dataviz form heuristic and both had to beat the sentence that would otherwise have replaced them.

Chart one — twelve weeks of finished work

Form: column chart, emphasis (one live mark in the accent, the rest in the de-emphasis grey). Job: change over time. It is a picture of: whether this is a good week or a turned corner.

Why it beats the sentence. “31 this month against a usual 22” gives the delta and hides the shape. Twelve columns tell you whether the number is a spike or a climb, and that distinction is the entire difference between “good month” and “I have turned this around.” A sentence cannot carry twelve values.

The honest detail that matters most: the current week is not over, so its column is drawn open-topped and hatched, labelled “so far”. A solid column there would claim a completed week. buildTrendPoints() would in fact drop that bucket entirely; drawing it as incomplete is more useful than dropping it, and equally honest.

Chart two — how long open work has been sitting

Form: one-dimensional dot strip. Job: distribution. It is a picture of: the tail.

Why it beats the sentence. On the prototype’s nine open jobs the median age is 9 days — a true sentence that hides the three sitting at 19, 23 and 41. The tail is the finding, and a median is the specific summary that destroys it. The strip also draws the fortnight threshold in place, so the count above it (“3 haven’t moved in over a fortnight”) is explained by the picture rather than asserted beside it.

What I deliberately did not chart

  • Open work over time. The most obvious analytics chart in existence, and it needs the snapshot writer. Refusing it is the honesty proof for everything else on the page — and it is drawn as an empty framed plot with its own sentence, rather than hidden.
  • A burndown or any forecast. There is no accepted baseline to burn down against. Drawing one would mean inventing the line.
  • A completion-rate percentage or gauge. A ratio hides its denominator, and this audience is not an analyst audience. Counts with the denominator beside them.
  • A sparkline in the hero. The twelve-week chart already carries the reading; a second miniature of it would be decoration.
  • Anything per-person. See workload_distribution above.

The categorical-colour collision, settled by measurement

A three-colour lock cannot seat a six-series legend. The brief asks me to solve this deliberately and write down the rule, because every future chart in the suite inherits it. So here is the rule, and here is the measurement that forced it.

Signal Analytics has no categorical palette. Identity is never carried by hue.

Magnitude is one hue (indigo, sequential). Emphasis is one indigo mark against zinc context. Identity — which label, which week, which job — is carried by position and direct label. Status is a reserved four that never doubles as a series colour.

The tempting cheat is to run categorical series off the ink ramp — zinc 700 / 500 / 400 / 300, four “colours” that stay inside the lock. It fails, and not marginally:

  • Emphasis pair #4f46e5 against #71717a: ΔE 20.1 protan, 22.0 normal, both above 3:1 on surface. The grey fails the chroma floor by design — it is the de-emphasis mark, not a series.
  • Sequential indigo for magnitude: usable from indigo 500 up. Indigo 300 measures 1.99:1 on white and indigo 400 measures 2.98:1, so either requires a visible label or the table view. Recorded so nobody re-derives it.
  • Every status mark ships with its word and its own shape — a dot, a rotated square, a ring. Nothing on this surface is distinguished by colour alone.

A design-system finding, measured in passing. The dark theme in ds-foundation/tokens/tokens.css does not redefine --status-flight, --status-blocked or --status-done. At their light values, #f59e0b (L 0.769) and #10b981 (L 0.696) both sit above the dark lightness band of 0.48–0.67.

Latent rather than live — the file states no product sets data-theme="dark" before launch. Worth a token pass before one does. Snapping each one step darker resolves it.

06

The delight, with verdicts

Two moments, in the catalogue’s own animate-or-restrained format (docs/DELIGHT_CATALOG.md). One of each, which is the point.

L1 · The previous-best line draws itself  Animate

When the current week is higher than any of the eleven before it, a dashed indigo rule draws left-to-right at the previous best and tags itself. 520ms, 340ms after paint, cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1); the tag fades in behind it.

The argument. This is the first time the product has ever congratulated the owner, and it does it with a fact rather than a flourish — the line is the previous number, drawn where it falls. The motion is what makes the reader understand that the bar passed something, which a static line at the same position does not communicate. It is the Timeline 79 days move — one expressive element carrying the whole composition — turned into something that can happen more than once.

The conditions. It draws only when the claim is true; there is no animation on an ordinary week. It resolves instantly and fully legibly under prefers-reduced-motion. It is the only motion on the surface, which is why it lands — BRAND §5.1’s “one earned celebration” logic, applied outside HQ.

L2 · The chart that is not there  Restrained

In movement three, the state trend the product cannot draw is not rendered as a grey box saying “no data”. It is rendered as the chart that would have been there — a dashed empty frame with its own faint gridlines and one sentence in the middle of the plot area: “The first reading is kept tonight. This fills in from tomorrow, one day at a time.”

The argument. The Plan Trace variant in Wave 5 found the right instinct — state the limit at the exact place the reader would otherwise assume the opposite — and this is that instinct given a whole device. A reader learns what the product does not know at the moment they are most likely to assume it does. It also converts the single most embarrassing gap in the domain into the most memorable thing on the page.

Verdict restrained, deliberately. No motion. The drawing is the moment; animating an absence would be the product performing its own limitation.

Candidates rejected

  • Count-up on the hero numeral. Restrained. HQ uses count-ups to say “this resolved from a live source”; here the number is a plain count and the motion would be decoration on the most important figure on the page.
  • Staggered entrance on the ages dots. Restrained. Family F4 (item arrival) has no shipped reference anywhere in the suite; inventing one here would set a register for a family nine other sites will inherit, which is a decision for the catalogue, not for this view.

Designed against the paid-for defect library

The elevate lessons name failure classes an analytics view walks straight into. Each is closed by construction rather than by review:

  • Self-contradicting counts. Every figure derives from one accessor object. The header’s “9 open”, the strip’s nine dots and the “3 haven’t moved” count are all computed from the same array — they cannot disagree.
  • Unanchored time. The reading time is stated on screen, and every relative phrase resolves against it.
  • State carried only visually. Every status mark carries its word; both charts carry a full sentence aria-label naming every value.
  • Loading that promises sections that never render. The skeleton is the real geometry of the real sections.
  • The undesigned edge. A derived axis maximum, so a 60-day job cannot fall off the end of its own chart; a knocked-out label so the record line never strikes through a value.

Phone

390px is the same composition, not a reduced one. Twelve columns still fit and stay legible — verified in the prototype at 390 — because the chart is narrow marks with a sparse axis rather than a dense grid. The hero numeral is the element that gains most from the narrow measure. The only things that change: the rail is replaced by the shell’s mobile nav, the “Open →” affordance drops (it does not exist on touch and the whole row is the target), and the axis labels wrap to two lines.

08

Data, privacy, and the finding that has to close

What it reads

Task rows and their activity for one Project, through the existing authorized providers. Timeline milestones for the same Project. Nothing else.

What it must never read

  • Note bodies. The Notes provider query names its columns and never names body; NoteRecord.exposure is approved_extract at contract level. This survives untouched.
  • Email. PersonRef deliberately excludes it.
  • Anything outside one Project. Every provider binds to one workspace and policy.ts revalidates exactly one membership. A portfolio total across four Projects would be a number nobody has proved the reader may see. The view states this as a first-class refusal rather than quietly scoping down.

The open security finding, and how this plan closes it. authorizeAnalyticsRequest returns a synthetic principal signal-${mode} for demo and review modes and never calls auth() (policy.ts:53–63). Preview deployments default to review (access-mode.ts:66). With the domain flag on, anyone holding a preview URL sees a fabricated portfolio. It is masked only because the flag 404s first.

Verified unchanged at fb9d3cbe.

This view must not inherit that posture, and the fix is not “keep the flag off” — deny-by-default by accident of a flag is not deny-by-default. Three changes, all in phase one:

  1. The Home Analytics route requires a real signed-in principal in every mode. Fixture data may be what is served in review mode; it is never what is served unauthenticated.
  2. On a non-production deployment the route additionally requires reviewer allowlisting — the posture the Wave 5 lab gate already built for itself (lib/analytics-lab/gate.ts) rather than a second invention.
  3. Failure is 404, never a redirect. A redirect confirms the route exists.

The Wave 5 lab index states the honest consequence of exactly this posture, and it applies here: those conditions make a surface unreachable on every environment this repo can currently deploy. Serving Lately from a protected preview needs a review posture that authenticates for real. That posture does not exist yet, and building it is phase one work, not a footnote.

09

The build sequence

Phase 1 — the view, on what is already true

Ships the whole composition using the seven real-now metrics. Depends on nothing that does not exist. Adds ANALYTICS_APP_PATH = "/app/home/analytics" to product-urls.ts beside BRIEFING_APP_PATH, the tab strip in Home, and the route behind SIGNAL_HOME_ANALYTICS_ENABLED — which already exists and already means the right thing.

Cannot be faked: the authentication fix in §08. It ships in this phase or the phase does not ship.

Can be faked for review: the data, through the existing fixture path — behind real authentication.

Phase 2 — switch on the recorders

Enable SIGNAL_ANALYTICS_SNAPSHOTS_ENABLED and SIGNAL_MILESTONE_DATE_HISTORY_ENABLED. Both are pure writers; neither changes a rendered surface. Do this as early as possible and independently of the view, because both accrue forward only — every day they stay off is a day of history that can never be recovered. This is the one item in the plan with a real deadline attached to doing nothing.

Phase 3 — the refusals start resolving

Roughly thirty days after phase 2, the state trend has enough snapshots to draw and the empty framed plot in movement three fills in — the same frame, now with marks in it, which is the payoff the ghost chart was designed to earn. Once a milestone date has moved since enablement, milestone_movement flips to reachable and its refusal card becomes a reading. Sixteen weeks after a workspace starts, completion_pace_change stops returning insufficient_history.

No new design is needed for any of this. The states are already built; they simply stop being the ones that render.

Phase 4 — elevate

Hand the surface to the loop. Not before phase 1 is real against real data — the lessons are explicit that a ledger cannot close over a surface that is still growing.

10

What I would cut, and did

The Label ledger

Wave 5’s Evidence Ledger is the best-argued of the five compositions: one table of every label in the Project, exceptions living inside it as its first rows rather than repeated above it, so reading order and completeness become one object. I liked it enough to build it before cutting it.

Why it goes. It answers “where is the work concentrated”, and that is not the question this tab exists for. It also requires the reader to hold a denominator caveat — labels overlap, so six rows sum to 12 across 9 items — which is exactly the kind of arithmetic the first-contact test says a newcomer should never be asked to do. The ledger is a good surface. It belongs inside Tasks, where labels live.

A period selector

Four weeks / twelve weeks / this year, as a control at the top. Every analytics surface has one and it is the single most obvious thing to build.

Why it goes. Changing the period silently changes the bucket size — buildTrendPoints switches from weekly to monthly above 93 days — so the same chart would mean two different things depending on a control the reader does not know is doing that. It would also let a reader select a period the data cannot cover and get a chart with holes in it. One fixed, well-chosen window that the copy can name precisely is more honest than a control that invites a question the data cannot answer. Revisit once snapshots have a year behind them.

Scope kept. No file under src/ was modified. No PR, no migration, no flag turned on, no existing artifact republished, and the elevate loop was not started. Work sits on design/home-analytics-2026-08, cut fresh from design/timeline-redesign-2026-08 at fb9d3cbe.

Where the evidence is thin. Every metric rating is read from the registry and every remediation claim is read from the code at HEAD — but nothing here has been run against a live production workspace, because no analytics surface reaches one. The twelve-week chart is derived from the machinery rather than observed from real output. That is the one claim in this plan I would want to see proven before phase 1 ships, and proving it is a day’s work: enable the domain flag on a properly authenticated preview and read one real workspace.
