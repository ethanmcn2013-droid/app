Signal Studio · Home · Lately · closing report

Three rounds took Lately from a surface that contradicted itself to one that does not, and the instrument now says stop.

Lately is the analytics view inside Signal Studio’s Home — one owner, one Project, looking back over twelve weeks to answer one question: am I actually getting on top of this, or just busy? This is the founder read on what three rounds of blind panel review did to it, what that cost, and what is still open.

The verdict

The ledger did not close. The engagement stopped on its declared budget of three rounds, with the distance published — which is what the method calls finished work.

Severity is clean and has been for two rounds: nothing blocking, nothing misleading. What is not clean is the cost of the repairs. Two thirds of what the closing round found was damage the earlier rounds’ own fixes had done. More rounds would not converge — they would keep finding the last round’s repairs.

What it was

A surface that disagreed with itself. The quiet state’s sentence and its own marks contradicted each other. The hero folded an unfinished week into a total it presented as complete. The error state stamped a successful reading at the moment of the failure. “Best week · 8” had never painted — in any state, on either ground, in any motion mode — because an animation’s fill outranked the rule meant to show it. Twenty-seven elements carried 748px of browser-default margin in seven values, none of them a rung on any ladder. And the primary reading gesture, space and page-down, moved nothing at all on a fresh load.

Every figure on the screen is now derived from the shipping fixture in src/lib/project-truth-fixture.ts, and the build fails if a state’s spoken sentence and its own marks disagree — the contradiction that opened round one is now impossible to reintroduce. Seven states are designed states, not fallbacks. The loading frame holds the real composition’s geometry and none of its facts, and will not even let you select the numbers it says it has not read.

Three of seven seats signed the work off at the close. The four who did not scored it between 8.6 and 9.1, against a benchmark set named to every seat by name: Linear, Stripe, Vercel, xAI/Grok, SpaceX.

Seventeen blind seat sittings and eighty-four adversarial refutations. 131 findings raised, 84 distinct claims, 64 confirmed and fixed, 20 refuted. Every confirmed finding was fixed before anything was re-scored, in batches of at most eight, with both gates run between batches — and closed only once something in the gate asserted it, written first and watched failing.

The refutation rate held between 22% and 26% across all three rounds. A quarter of everything seven expert seats filed did not survive one fresh reader instructed to default to refuted. That number is the reason the panel is worth running and the reason no finding goes straight to a fix.

What is still open

On the surface — one item, severity defect

The chart’s eleven context columns sit at 1.48:1 on paper and 1.70:1 on the dark twin, under the 3:1 non-text contrast floor. It is unfixed because the obvious fix inverts the chart: the current week is a hatch compositing to 1.71:1, fainter than the columns it would be contrasted against. Darkening the context alone makes the live mark the weakest thing in the frame. This needs a design decision, not a token swap.

Five further items are on the port packet as things that do not exist yet rather than things done badly: a destination for a filtered list of open work, a wired retry, per-column values that would not cost twelve tab stops, a bound record-start instant, and the chart-wide answer above. The surface deliberately offers no affordance that leads nowhere — each ships when its destination does.

And one thing that is not about the pixels at all: nobody owns the port. Three prior engagements ran forty-five rounds, raised 1,358 findings and made 919 fixes, and shipped zero pixels. This engagement hands over a port packet a separate build session lands. If that session is not named, this makes four.

The recommendation

Stop the loop and start the port. The panel has stopped finding the product and started finding itself: seven of the closing round’s twenty-one confirmed findings were assertions written in earlier rounds that could not fail for the thing they named. That is a real result — the gate is stronger for it — but it is not progress on Lately, and a fourth round would buy more of the same.

The one open surface item is a decision only you can make, and it is cheaper to make it against real data during the port than against a fixture in the lab.
