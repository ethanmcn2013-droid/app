Tasks Elevation Log

Signal Studio · Tasks · Studio Floor · August 2026

Taking a locked design to a gate

The direction is settled and is not being redesigned. This is the record of the elevation: the palette locked to three colours, then round after round of an independent seven-seat panel scoring the work against the standard of an award-winning studio that iterated on it for months. Every finding is put to a fresh agent whose job is to refute it, so only what survives gets fixed.

Executive summary

What this cost, what it bought, and where it stopped

Nineteen rounds of an independent panel, 639 findings raised and 421 fixed, every one of them put to a fresh agent whose job was to refute it. The work is being stopped at roughly 8.4 against a 9.5 gate. That number needs its caveat stated plainly, and the reason it is not higher is worth more than the number itself.

The final round returned a floor of 7.3, and that figure is real but not representative: it was set by a single seat, on a defect introduced by the previous round's own remediation and fixed immediately after. The other six seats returned 8.2, 8.3, 8.3, 8.4, 8.4 and 8.6, and the round before it ran 8.2 to 8.7 across all seven. 8.4 is the settled reading of the artifact; 7.3 is the reading of a mistake. Both are in the record below, unedited.

The finding that matters

The work was never the constraint. The loop was. From round 9 onward the panel stopped finding the same defects getting smaller and started finding new defects in whatever had been built since the last panel. Across rounds 12 to 15 that accounted for 53%, 39%, 54% and 68% of each round's confirmed defect cost — rising, not falling.

Freezing the surface stopped the decline and halved the cost per round, but it did not produce a climb: four frozen rounds read 8.3, 8.2, 8.2, then 7.3. What moved the floor in the last two rounds was not the artifact at all — it was defects introduced by the remediation itself, twice in a row, each caught only by the next full round. That is the point at which the loop stopped grading the product and started grading its own repairs, and it is the reason this stops here rather than at round twenty.

That is the real ceiling. A 9.5 unanimous bar is an AND across seven expert lenses where the score is the lowest seat: it requires a state in which nobody, across seven different ways of looking, can find anything worth more than a few tenths — on a sweep that reliably produces 25 to 35 findings. No round of nineteen produced fewer than 16 confirmed defects. The bar as written is reachable only by an artifact that has stopped changing — and each attempt to close the last point cost a full round to discover an error the author could not see.

What was bought. The score is the least valuable thing this produced. The durable assets are a 360-assertion behaviour gate that drives the real board rather than reading it, an audit that reads rendered pixels rather than declared values, a preflight that refuses to start a round when the frames are stale or the app has drifted from its master, and a written record of every finding and every refutation. Three defects found here had been live and invisible for more than ten rounds — including a drag threshold that silently swallowed the board's primary gesture on ordinary trackpad hardware, invisible to 232 assertions because every one of them clicked with a teleport.

06 · shipping it

Getting three elevated products into production

Tasks, Notes and Timeline are being elevated in three separate sessions. The question is how they reach production. This is the recommendation and the reasoning, written by the session that did Tasks and made the mistakes.

Do not give all three to one session

It is the obvious answer and it is the wrong one. This session's own record is the argument: on the two occasions a large change was landed in a single pass, the change introduced a defect its author could not see, and each one cost a full review round to find. One agent integrating three products at once is that failure mode at three times the surface area, with no panel between the mistake and production.

Equally, do not simply run three merges one after another. The three products share their chrome — the spine, the dock, the floor, the motion contract and the token layer are one system across all of them. Three sessions each merging their own version of a shared surface produces three incompatible answers, and whichever lands last silently overwrites the other two.

Reconcile the shared chrome, alone, before any product ships

One short session whose only job is the surface all three touch: the spine, the dock, the ink floor, the motion tokens and the radius and shadow ladders. It ships nothing else. This is the only part that genuinely cannot be parallelised, and it is small.

  • Read all three masters, list every place they disagree about a shared object, and resolve each disagreement once.
  • Rewrite check-chrome-contract.mjs and board-pass3-contract.test.mjs to the settled answer — both are knowingly unmet today and both need one coherent rewrite, not three.
  • Merge that alone. Nothing else moves until it is on main.

Port one product, in verified increments, and prove the pipeline

Tasks first, because it has the gate. Not as one drop: the port is a translation, not a copy. The lab master is a single file with its own render loop; the app is React over server state. The CSS transfers mechanically and the behaviour does not — that is exactly how this project shipped a stacked-header stylesheet with nothing to stamp it, and left the defect it was meant to fix live in production.

  • Run extract-floor-css.mjs for the stylesheet, then port each behaviour by hand and assert it.
  • Put extract-floor-css.mjs --check in CI. Master-to-app drift reached 209 lines here, silently, because re-running it was a thing somebody had to remember.
  • Make floor-prod-check.mjs a merge gate, so the app is held to the assertions the lab is held to.

Port the other two against a pipeline that is already proven

Now they can run in parallel or in sequence, and it stops mattering which: the contested surface is decided, the contracts are rewritten, and the drift check is in CI. Each product session integrates only its own product.

Elevate the shipped app, not the lab

Once a product is in production its master has done its job. Run the panel against the running app from then on, or the lab and the app drift apart again — which is precisely what happened here between rounds 11 and 12, and it cost a round to discover.

The prompt

What to give the chrome session

Start a session in the worktree, in plan mode, and give it this. It is deliberately narrow: it forbids product work, because the whole point of the phase is that the shared surface is settled before anything else moves.

Do not port a product. Do not touch a board, a note list or a timeline. If a
change would improve one product and not the other two, it is out of scope and
belongs to that product's own session.

What to produce:
1. A written list of every place the three masters disagree about a shared
   object, with the disagreement stated in one line each.
2. One resolution per disagreement, argued from the direction lock and the
   panel records rather than from preference. Where two masters were panelled
   to different conclusions on the same object, say so and pick, giving the
   reason.
3. The reconciled chrome, implemented once.
4. check-chrome-contract.mjs and board-pass3-contract.test.mjs rewritten to the
   settled answer. Both are knowingly unmet today and the direction lock records
   that they need rewriting at implementation. They get one rewrite, not three.
5. Assertions for every resolution, in the style of the existing gate: drive the
   real thing with real pointer travel, never locator.click(), and state the
   class-level rule each assertion protects rather than the instance.

Hard-won rules from the Tasks elevation, which cost nineteen rounds to learn:
- Fix the rule, not the instance. A defect class fixed on three selectors came
  back on a fourth; a guard fixed on one control came back on its sibling.
- No attribute a measure pass writes may participate in the layout that same
  pass measures.
- The CSS transfers mechanically and the behaviour does not. A stylesheet ported
  without its behaviour ships dead rules and leaves the defect live.
- Land it in small verified increments. Every large single-pass change in that
  engagement introduced a defect its author could not see.

Do not merge anything else while this is open, and do not start a product port
until this is on main.
One thing to decide before any of it. The 9.5 gate was written against an artifact at rest and has been applied to a growing product. Decide now whether production ships at the bar the panel actually certified — Tasks at 8.4, with the resting board scoring consistently higher than the whole — or whether the gate is re-scoped to the resting board and the rest reviewed but not gating. It is a founder decision, it takes a sentence, and leaving it open is what lets a shipped product be judged against a bar nobody agreed applied to it.

07 · the set

Four documents describe this one piece of work

The board itself, the record of how it got here, the argument about where it stopped, and the critique of the method that took it there. Each is on every one of the others.

How the panel works

Seven seats run in parallel with no knowledge of one another: UI composition, typography, interaction and states, UX and information design, brand and copy, product taste, and one seat that distrusts opinion and reads the source. Every finding a seat raises is then handed to a fresh agent whose only job is to refute it, and which defaults to refused when uncertain.

The measured seat is automated and runs on every change: palette lock, type weights, families, WCAG AA contrast against the real composited backdrop, hit targets including pseudo-element expanders, the radii ladder and the motion tokens, across five states. It has passed every check since the palette was locked, which is why the panel's findings are about things automation cannot see.

Exploration only. Local branch design/tasks-exploration, no pull request and no deploy.
