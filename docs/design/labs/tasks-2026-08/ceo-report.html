The 9.5 Question

Fifteen rounds. Seven blind seats each time, one adversarial refuter per finding, 525 findings raised and 336 fixed. The floor peaked at 8.6 in round 9 and sits at 7.9 today. This is why, what I would do about it, and the loop I have rebuilt to do it.

Prepared 24 August 2026 · rounds 1–15 · lowest seat is the score

The work is not the problem. The loop is. Since round 9, the seats have not been finding the same defects getting smaller — they have been finding new defects in whatever was built since the last panel. In round 15, 68% of all confirmed defect cost came from one room that did not exist two rounds earlier.

A 9.5 unanimous gate is an AND across seven independent expert judgments, where the score is the lowest seat. It is reachable only by an artifact that has stopped changing. We have been running it against a product that changes every round.

Where the number actually went

The arc has three distinct phases, and reading them as one line is what makes the current score look like failure.

What changed at round 9

Nothing about the panel. The refute rate has been steady at 36% for the whole engagement — the seats are not getting softer or harsher, and the refuters are killing roughly the same share of findings they always did.

What changed is that we started shipping new capability between panels. Up to round 9, remediation mostly removed defects from a fixed surface. After it, remediation began adding surface: a header band, a rebuilt empty state, a locked card preset, an entire Planning room. Every addition arrives unpanelled, and the next panel finds it.

The UX seat put it in one sentence at round 15, without being asked to diagnose anything: "The score is held down by one room shipped since the last panel, not by fourteen rounds of work."

The gate is stricter than it reads

"9.5 unanimous, the score is the lowest seat" sounds like one bar. It is seven. A seat's score is anchored by its own worst finding, so a 9.5 floor requires a state in which no seat, across seven different lenses, can find anything worth more than a few tenths — on a sweep that reliably produces 35 findings a round.

Fifteen rounds have produced no round with fewer than 22 confirmed defects. That is not a sign of bad work; it is what seven expert lenses do to any artifact of this size. The gate as written is reachable only by something that has stopped moving.

Every seat has been at or above 8.7 at least once. No two seats have ever peaked in the same round. That is the whole problem in one line: the craft is there, but it is never all present at the same moment, because each round's remediation disturbs a different surface.

What I would do

Three real options. They are not mutually exclusive, but the first one is the decision that matters.

A · Freeze the surface, then converge

Recommended

Declare a feature freeze on the master for three rounds. Remediation may only remove defects — no new rooms, no new controls, no new capability. Anything a seat asks to build goes on a list for after the gate instead of into the next round.

The evidence says this is worth roughly a point on its own. Strip the Planning-attributable findings out of round 15 and the UX seat — the one that set the 7.9 — has almost nothing left in its lens.

Cost
Three rounds, no new product surface
Expected
Floor 8.8–9.2 within three rounds
Risk
Planning and Schedule stay unbuilt for longer

B · Split the gate in two

Do alongside A

The 9.5 bar was set against an artifact — a board at rest. It is being applied to a growing product. Split it: 9.5 on the resting board (the six shot states, which is what the founder objection at the direction lock was actually about), and a separate 9.0 floor on everything else, reviewed but not gating.

This is not lowering the bar. It is naming which surface the bar was for, so that shipping a new room stops invalidating a score the resting board already earned.

Cost
One founder decision, no engineering
Expected
The resting board is likely within one round of 9.5
Risk
Two numbers to track instead of one

C · Ship at the current bar and move to Notes

Viable

Every seat has independently called the resting board finished work at Linear and Vercel standard. Interaction called the keyboard model "at or above Linear". The remaining distance is, in the seats' own estimates, one to four days of polish — not a rethink.

Tasks is one of three products. Notes and Timeline have had no elevation at all. There is a real argument that the marginal point on Tasks is worth less than the first six points on Notes.

Cost
Accept 8.2–8.6 on Tasks
Expected
Two more products reach 8.5 in the time Tasks reaches 9.2
Risk
The 9.5 bar stops meaning anything if it is waived once

The recommendation

Take A and B together, and put C on the calendar.

Freeze the master's feature surface for rounds 16 to 18 and run pure convergence rounds. At the same time, split the gate so the resting board is graded as the artifact it is. My expectation, on the arc so far, is that the resting board clears 9.5 in two or three rounds and the whole-product floor settles around 9.0.

Then make the C decision deliberately rather than by drift: with Tasks at a defensible bar and a working method, the next marginal point is almost certainly worth more spent on Notes.

One thing to keep whatever you choose

The infrastructure built during these rounds is worth more than the score. There is now a preflight that refuses to start a round when the frames are stale or the app has drifted from the master, an audit that reads rendered pixels rather than declared values, and a behaviour gate that has grown from 186 assertions to 299 — every one of them added because a seat found the defect it guards. Three defects this engagement found had been live and invisible for more than ten rounds, including a drag threshold that silently swallowed the board's primary gesture on ordinary trackpad hardware. That does not come back if the loop stops.

The new loop, in operation

Recommendation A and B are built and pushed. Five changes, each answering a specific failure the record shows rather than a hunch.

1 · The surface is frozen

A finding whose answer is "build X" is now marked build, recorded, deferred to a list for after the gate, and never remediated during a round. Seats are told the freeze exists so they do not spend findings on it. This is the change that addresses the 68%.

2 · Rules, not patches

Every finding must now carry a class-level rule, phrased so it could be checked mechanically, and the refuter judges the rule as well as the fix. Preflight fails if a round is recorded whose remediation did not grow the gate. This is the answer to the same defect class recurring three times: fixed on three preset rules, found on a fourth; fixed on the tick, found on the card body.

3 · Seats are handed what is already proven

The gate's claims now go into the brief by name. 189 findings were refuted over fifteen rounds, many of them re-raising settled ground. Nine kilobytes of assertion names is the cheapest fix available to this loop.

4 · Verification is spent where it decides the score

Findings at or above 1.0 keep their own adversarial refuter. Everything below is batched per seat, judged independently. A 0.15 typography note cannot move a floor and was costing a full agent to refute.

5 · Headroom

Every seat now reports the score it would give if its own findings were fixed exactly as written. That is a prediction the next round checks. If a round's floor lands well below the previous round's predicted floor, the loop is churning, not converging — and we will know in one round instead of three.

What it costs

Converge rounds run five seats: the four that have set the floor most often, plus one rotating so no lens goes unwatched. Gate rounds run all seven, and only when a converge round leaves no seat under 9.3, because a unanimous bar cannot be certified by a subset. Roughly 18 agents a round against 42, and 26 frames instead of 164 now that only the locked configuration is shot.

What I expect. Three frozen convergence rounds, 16 to 18, then a gate round. On the arc so far I expect the floor at 8.8–9.2 by round 18 and the resting board through 9.5 shortly after. If round 16's floor comes in below round 15's predicted headroom, the diagnosis was wrong and I will say so rather than run three more rounds to find out.
