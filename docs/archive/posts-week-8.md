### Tue 06-30, 8:00pm ET — r/productivity (long discussion) — refusal list, post-launch
- [ ] Post

**Title:** Six weeks of public reaction to a published refusal list — what the productivity audience actually wants

**Body:**

> Solo founder, productivity tool at tasks-nu-hazel.vercel.app, just finished an eight-week launch cycle with two beats — Show HN and Product Hunt. The single piece of writing that outperformed everything else was a page called `/principles` — eight features I've publicly committed to never shipping.
>
> The refusal list, in short:
> - No per-seat pricing
> - No Gantt charts
> - No SSO as a marketing line
> - No AI agent that runs your tasks for you
> - No real-time push notifications
> - No story points / velocity / burndown / OKR
> - No paid template marketplace
> - No threaded comments-on-comments
>
> Six weeks of public reaction taught me three things I didn't know going in.
>
> **First: the refusal list is the audience filter, not the product positioning.** I shipped it thinking it would explain what Tasks is. It did the opposite — it explained who Tasks is for. Engineers from 200-person SaaS orgs read the list and bounced. Wedding planners, college students, freelancers, restaurant GMs, school admins — the audience I actually built for — read the list and stayed. The refusal list does the qualifying conversation that a sales call would do at a different price point.
>
> **Second: the most-contested item is not the one I expected.** I assumed the no-AI-agent line would draw the most pushback. It drew some, but the single most-contested refusal was *no real-time push notifications.* People are conditioned to expect a productivity tool to ping them. Removing the ping reads as a feature gap until you sit with it for a week, at which point it reads as the actual product. The signal: distribution-by-attention is so deeply baked into category expectations that even the audience asking for calm tools doesn't know what calm tools look like at first contact.
>
> **Third: a published refusal list is a contract you can be held to, and that's exactly the point.** Three different commenters across HN, PH, and IH cited the list back to me when asking for features I'd refused. *"The principles page says no Gantt, but what about a slim timeline view?"* The list works because it's load-bearing — every time I'm asked for one of the eight, the page does the no for me. The voice integrity holds because the refusal isn't a tactical choice, it's a published commitment.
>
> The thing I'd hand to anyone building a tool in this category: name what you refuse, on a public URL, before you launch. The refusal does more positioning work than any feature list you can write. And it gets sharper, not weaker, every time someone asks you to break it.
>
> Full list at tasks-nu-hazel.vercel.app/principles. Curious what the productivity-power-user audience here would add to the list — what's the one feature you wish a tool would publicly refuse to build?
