**Title:** Built a productivity app where inviting people doesn't cost extra — pricing math that broke

**Body:**

> Solo founder, no funding. Shipped a productivity tool called Tasks: tasks-nu-hazel.vercel.app.
>
> The whole thing turns on one pricing call: per workspace, not per seat. A wedding planner shouldn't pay more to invite the florist. A study group shouldn't pay more to add the fourth member. Inviting people becomes a free action instead of a budget decision.
>
> The pricing today:
> - Free — 1 workspace, 3 editing guests, all 4 views
> - Pro — $4.99/mo, unlimited workspaces (one user)
> - Team — $9.95/workspace/mo, unlimited members
> - Studio — $14.95/mo, unlimited workspaces you own as sole admin (the operator tier)
> - Wedding — $79 once, lifetime, for the workspace that matters most
>
> Where the math broke first: freelancers running 5 clients on Team would hit 5 × $9.95 a month. Wedding planners running 10 weddings/year would hit 10 × $79. Both audiences would either bounce or downgrade to Pro and lose Team features. Studio is the patch — one user-level entitlement row, layered server-side, unlimited workspaces under one roof.
>
> No per-seat upsell. No SSO upsell. No AI agent that runs your tasks for you. Eight features published as a refusal list at /principles.
>
> Open to questions on the pricing, the views, the refusal list, anything. The whole codebase is open and the /changelog reads itself.

### Fri 05-22, 9:00am ET — Indie Hackers (cross-post)
- [ ] Post

**Title:**

> Why we charge per workspace, not per seat — and the math behind it

**Body:**

> We launched a productivity tool a few months ago [tasks-nu-hazel.vercel.app]. The single biggest call we made was per-workspace pricing instead of per-seat. Here's what that means in practice — and where the math held up vs. broke.
>
> The pricing today:
> - Free — 1 workspace, 3 editing guests
> - Pro — $4.99/mo, unlimited workspaces (for one user)
> - Team — $9.95/workspace/mo, unlimited members in that workspace
> - Studio — $14.95/mo, unlimited workspaces YOU own (for operators)
> - Wedding — $79 once, lifetime, for the workspace that matters most
>
> The thinking: per-seat pricing makes inviting people a budget decision, which kills group adoption. We'd rather a wedding workspace fit the bride, groom, both moms, MOH, and DJ for $79 lifetime than charge them 6× anything to do the same thing. Inviting the florist should not be a line item.
>
> What worked:
> - The wedding workspace is the clearest product-market fit we've found. Couples buy the $79 tier without needing convincing — it's a price they can read in one beat against the rest of a wedding budget.
> - Three free editing guests on every workspace, *including the free tier*, cut our paid-conversion friction in a way we didn't expect. Study groups, couples, freelancer-and-client pairs all fit free. The free tier earns its keep — it's a real product, not a teaser.
> - Plain-English vertical pricing. *Wedding* is a tier. Not a "Plus" or "Premium" — a *Wedding.* Once we named the tier after the use case, conversion on the pricing page jumped.
>
> What broke:
> - Freelancers running 5 clients hit 5× $9.95 immediately on Team — $49.75/mo for what's structurally one operator's work. They were bouncing.
> - Wedding planners running 10 weddings/year would need 10× $79 to use the product they already knew. Same problem at a different scale.
> - Both audiences are *operators* — one person running multiple discrete workspaces. Per-workspace pricing assumes the workspace and the buyer are roughly co-extensive. For operators, they aren't.
>
> The fix was a new tier: Studio at $14.95/mo, unlimited workspaces the user owns as sole admin, full Team capabilities on every one. We modeled it as a single user-level entitlement row that layers on top of the per-workspace lookup — `workspaceId IS NULL`, tier `studio`. No bulk INSERTs at purchase time. No cleanup INSERTs when a new workspace is created. No DELETEs on cancellation — the existing `expiresAt` mechanic handles that. One row, two query updates, done.
>
> `TIER_RANK[studio] === TIER_RANK[team]`. They unlock the same features; they just have different scope. Same gating code, no branch.
>
> The lesson I'd hand to anyone building per-tenant pricing: name your "operator" persona before you launch. They're the audience that breaks per-tenant math, and they're easier to find than you think — anyone whose business shape is *one of me, many of you* is an operator. Freelancers, wedding planners, contractors, consultants, agency owners.
>
> Full pricing math + the reasoning is at tasks-nu-hazel.vercel.app/pricing. The full cycle 21 narrative — Studio's architecture, the Stripe sentinel for null workspaceId, the entitlement-resolution query — is at tasks-nu-hazel.vercel.app/changelog.
>
> What's your per-tenant pricing edge case? What broke when you launched it, and how did you fix it?
