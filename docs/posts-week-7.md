> - Published `/p/{slug}` workspaces — [`[NEEDS-REVIEW: insert published workspace count Wed 06-24 8am ET]`]
>
> The single biggest call we made was per-workspace pricing instead of per-seat. The pitch: inviting people becomes a free action instead of a budget decision. A wedding workspace fits the bride, groom, both moms, the MOH, and the DJ for one number. A study group fits the lab partner and the advisor for the same number. Per-workspace, not per-head.
>
> The pricing today:
>
> - Free — 1 workspace, 3 editing guests
> - Pro — $4.99/mo, unlimited workspaces (one user)
> - Team — $9.95/workspace/mo, unlimited members
> - Studio — $14.95/mo, unlimited workspaces YOU own (operators)
> - Wedding — $79 once, lifetime
>
> What worked at launch — the wedding tier closes without a sales call (the launch-week mix on $79 was [`[NEEDS-REVIEW: insert wedding-tier purchase count Wed 06-24 8am ET]`]). Three free editing guests on the free tier cut paid-conversion friction in a way we didn't model in advance. Naming the tier after the use case (*Wedding,* not *Premium*) measurably outperformed generic naming on the pricing-page click test we ran in cycle 18.
>
> What broke — the operator persona. Freelancers running 5 clients hit 5 × $9.95 immediately on Team. Wedding planners running 10 weddings/year would need 10 × $79. They were bouncing in cycle 21, before we patched it. The fix was Studio at $14.95/mo — a single user-level entitlement row that layers on top of the per-workspace lookup. No bulk INSERTs at purchase. No DELETEs on cancellation. `TIER_RANK[studio] === TIER_RANK[team]`, same gating code, no branch.
>
> What surprised me about the launch week — the refusal list traveled further than the pricing did. The single most-quoted line in HN comments and PH comments wasn't *per-workspace pricing,* it was *no AI agent that runs your tasks for you. The dopamine of crossing it off is not outsourceable.* Positioning by what you refuse to build is rarer than positioning by what you build, and rarity reads as honesty.
>
> The lesson I'd hand to anyone building per-tenant pricing: name your *operator persona* before you launch. Anyone whose business shape is *one of me, many of you* is an operator. They're the audience that breaks per-tenant math. Patch them with a single user-level entitlement row, not a new schema.
>
> Full pricing math is at tasks-nu-hazel.vercel.app/pricing. Full refusal list is at tasks-nu-hazel.vercel.app/principles. Eight months of cycle narratives — including the cycle 21 Studio-tier writeup — are at tasks-nu-hazel.vercel.app/changelog.
>
> One question for the room: what's the equivalent of an *operator persona* in your product? The customer whose use case looks like a normal one but whose math breaks the unit of pricing you started with?
