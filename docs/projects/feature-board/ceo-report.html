The case

You asked for a feature you already shipped

Three findings from a scan of this repo, in order of how much they cost. None of them are opinions — each one is a file you can open.

Finding 1 · we under-claim

Nudge is live. It has been for months.

src/lib/nudges/generate-nudges.ts holds seven rules — idle‑doing, idle‑review, past‑due, blocker‑cleared, review‑pile, doing‑empty, and an LLM narration slot. It runs server-side on /app/inbox against your real workspace column config, and again on /app/my-tasks. Dismissals persist. The copy bank has multiple variants per rule so the inbox doesn't read identically week over week.

You described it as a feature that could be cool. That gap — between what is built and what the person who built it believes is built — is the whole argument for this project.

Finding 2 · we over-claim

The marketing site sells presence the app does not have.

src/components/marketing/features.tsx promises "cursors carry weight, cards lock when someone's in them, comments stream in with typing indicators." In the app: there is no card locking anywhere, no typing indicator, and CursorsLayer is imported by exactly one file — the marketing hero's own animation.

Realtime itself is real but deliberately off. src/app/api/events/route.ts says so in a comment: the in-process emitter only fans out inside one lambda, so "the SSE stream stays off in production so we don't ship a realtime UX that silently no-ops." That was a good call. Nobody told the marketing page.

Finding 3 · we lose things in between

Dependencies are half-shipped and nothing says so.

blockedBy is in the schema, feeds the analytics provider, and drives the briefing's dependency-stall trigger with five locked phrasings. But src/components/app/blockers/blocker-badge.tsx has no importer at all. The data knows a task is blocked; the briefing will tell you at 7am; the board will not show it.

No status field would have caught this. Only evidence catches it.

Two directions of drift, plus a feature stuck between rungs. A board that only records what someone typed into a status field would have caught none of the three.

That is the gap. The feature board is not a visualisation project that produces a page. It is a consolidation project that happens to produce a page worth looking at.

The idea

Maturity, not a pipeline

Every roadmap board in the market sorts by when. Dates are the part of a roadmap that is always wrong, and everyone reading one knows it. Sort by how real instead, and the board stops being a promise and starts being a statement of fact.

RungMeansProven by
ConsideredWritten down and argued. No code.A decision record. Nothing else.
BuildingCode exists. A customer cannot reach it.Files present, route absent or flag closed.
LiveA customer can reach it today.Route in the manifest, dispatch entry naming it.
ProvenLive, and carrying evidence.Tests bound to it, a council score, or measured use.
CutTried, refused, or removed.A cuts entry in the dispatch.

Keep the Cut lane. It is the whole differentiator.

Every public roadmap is a wish list. Almost none of them publish what the team refused. This one can: the dispatch already carries eight cuts entries, and the brand already publishes a refusal list at /principles. "We built this and took it out, here is why" is the single most credible thing a product page can say, and nobody in the category is saying it. It is also the cheapest lane to fill — the entries already exist.

Colour is not the encoding.

The board redesign in T·149 removed the amber lane dot, the green tick and the red overdue line on purpose — "it says which of those it is with ink, and never with a colour." This board holds that line. Maturity is a fill ramp: hairline, then ink, then the one indigo tick for Proven. It survives greyscale, colour blindness, and print, and it looks like the rest of the product rather than like a dashboard someone bolted on.

The mechanism

A hand-maintained board is a lie with a six-week fuse

In a repo that machine-checks nine contracts on every commit, a board maintained by remembering to update it would be the only surface with no gate. It would be beautiful in week one and quietly wrong by October.

So the registry is source, and a gate reads it:

GateFails when
Route truthA feature claims live and its route is not in check-route-manifest.mjs.
Orphan checkA feature names a file that has no importer — the blocker-badge case.
Dispatch bindingA ships entry names no feature id, or names one that does not exist.
Marketing truthThe marketing features page claims something not at live or above.
StalenessA feature sits at building for 90 days with no dispatch entry.

The staleness rule is the important one, and it is the one everybody leaves out. Registries do not rot because entries are wrong at the moment they are written. They rot because nobody is ever forced to look again. Ninety days of silence should break the build, and the fix should be allowed to be "this is cut" — recording a refusal must be as easy as recording a ship, or the board fills with polite fiction.

Architecture

One registry, five consumers

This is what turns a page into infrastructure. The board is the first thing to read the registry, not the only one — and the second reader is what pays for the first.

content/features/<id>.md One file per feature. Front matter: id, name, lane, rung, visibility, gate, routes, evidence paths, dispatch ids. Body: what it is, in the voice a customer reads.
The board Internal now, public when earned. Generated at build.
Marketing features Stops being six hardcoded cards. Cannot claim what is not live.
Pricing & gates Each tier lists the features it actually unlocks, by id.
The dispatch Every ships entry binds to a feature id. History becomes queryable.
Signal HQ sync The AGENTS.md sync table becomes mechanical instead of remembered.

There is a sixth reader worth naming: the next person or agent to open this repo. "Where does capture-by-email live?" is currently a twenty-minute archaeology problem. With the registry it is one file lookup. That alone probably repays the build.

Experience

Delight, with verdicts written down

The north star says delight is deliberate and every candidate moment gets an explicit animate-or-restrained verdict. Here are the eight candidates on this surface, decided in advance rather than sprinkled afterwards. Five are refusals.

Promotion between rungs Animate The moment worth spending on. A feature that moved since your last visit arrives having just moved, with a hairline trail from where it was. Once per feature, ever.

Utility

Four readers, one page

A surface that needs explaining is not done. This one is a list of things with a mark next to each saying how real it is — which passes the first-contact test without a glossary, in any of the four readings below.

  • You, now. What do I have, what is half-built, what did I refuse. Answered by the distribution strip before you read a single row.
  • A collaborator or an agent. Where does this live in the code. Answered by the receipt.
  • A prospect, later. Is any of this real. Answered by the fact that every claim is clickable — which is what a public roadmap has never once been able to say.
  • The repo. CI reads it, marketing reads it, pricing reads it, HQ syncs from it.

Risks and refusals

Three ways this goes wrong

RiskWhat it looks likeThe refusal
It becomes a second backlog Dates appear. Then owners. Then priorities. Then it is Tasks, worse. The board records state, never plans. No dates, no assignees, no priorities, ever. docs/founder-todos.md already carries this exact warning about itself.
It exposes the roadmap A competitor reads what you have not built yet. Per-feature visibility, filtered at generation. Internal entries never reach the client bundle.
The rungs get gamed Everything is "Live" because Live looks better than Building. The rung is derived from evidence, not typed. If the route is missing, the gate fails and the claim does not ship.

One more, quietly: this is the second time a "single source of truth" has been proposed for this material — HQ was the first, and the sync is still a table in a contributing guide that a human has to remember. If the board does not make that sync mechanical, it is a third disagreeing system rather than a fix. That is the bar I would hold it to.

What I need from you

Four decisions

Registry in this repo, or in Signal HQ?
Recommend: this repo. The evidence lives here — routes, tests, dispatch. A registry that cannot see its own evidence cannot be gated. HQ syncs from it, as it does for everything else.
Does the Cut lane go public?
Recommend: yes, and first. It is the most credible and least copyable thing on the page, and the entries already exist. If it makes you uncomfortable, that is usually the signal that it is the interesting one.
Does the marketing features page start reading the registry?
Recommend: yes, in phase 3. It is the surface that drifted furthest, and it is the one with a customer on the other end of it.
Realtime presence — fix the app, or fix the claim?
Recommend: fix the claim now, decide the app separately. The marketing copy can be true this week. Whether presence is worth a substrate is a real product decision and should not be made under the pressure of an inaccurate sentence.
